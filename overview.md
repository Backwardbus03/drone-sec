## Objective 1: Drone Forensics Toolkit Development

### 1.1 Objective

The objective of this Grand Challenge is to design, develop, and validate an indigenous Drone Forensic Toolkit capable of acquiring, preserving, analyzing, and reporting digital evidence from Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and their associated components in a forensically sound manner. The toolkit should support evidence acquisition from onboard storage, flight controllers, remote controllers, removable media, companion mobile applications, and other relevant data sources while ensuring integrity through cryptographic hashing. The solution should enable automated data extraction, timeline reconstruction, flight path analysis, metadata extraction, and comprehensive forensic reporting, and should be scalable, extensible, and suitable for deployment by law enforcement agencies, forensic laboratories, regulatory authorities, and defence organizations for investigation of drone-related incidents.

### 1.2 Problem Statement

Despite the growing importance of drone forensics, the investigation process remains challenging due to diverse hardware architectures, proprietary data formats, encrypted storage, vendor-specific communication protocols, volatile memory, and the absence of standardized forensic acquisition and analysis methodologies. These challenges make it difficult to preserve evidence integrity and perform comprehensive forensic investigations across different UAV platforms.

The toolkit should support the acquisition of digital evidence from onboard storage, removable storage media, flight controllers, and remote controllers. The proposed solution should incorporate secure evidence preservation mechanisms, cryptographic hashing for integrity verification, chain-of-custody management, automated evidence parsing, timeline reconstruction, and standardized forensic reporting suitable for investigative and legal proceedings. The toolkit should also demonstrate repeatable and verifiable forensic acquisition processes under controlled test conditions.

Drone Forensic Toolkit system should be modular, scalable, vendor-independent, and easily extensible to support emerging UAV technologies, new drone platforms, and evolving forensic requirements with minimal modifications. The ultimate goal is to establish an indigenous, reliable, and standardized drone forensic capability that can support law enforcement agencies, regulatory bodies, forensic laboratories, defense organizations, and cybersecurity professionals in conducting efficient and legally defensible drone investigations.

### 1.3 Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Successful forensic acquisition from multiple drone platforms | 20% |
| Evidence integrity using hashing and chain-of-custody | 20% |
| Ability to recover logs, telemetry, GPS, media, and configuration files | 20% |
| Completeness of forensic reporting | 15% |
| Automation and usability of forensic toolkit | 10% |
| Extensibility to new drone platforms | 5% |
| Documentation and reproducibility | 5% |
| Future integration potential | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

**Final evaluation will be based on forensic acquisition, evidence preservation, repeatability, reporting quality, end-to-end investigation capability and viva during the Techfest evaluation.**

### 1.4 Structure

The Grand Challenge will be conducted over approximately 4 months and will culminate in final presentations at IIT Bombay Techfest in December 2026.

> **Note:** Teams can participate in either of the Objectives which will be evaluated as per the details provided. At the end of the challenge, up to 5 winning teams will be selected for Grand Challenge 3. These teams will be considered for further support, funding, mentoring, and access to resources for building and testing their designs in the next phase.

#### Stage 1: Problem Statement Announcement and Preliminary Design

The Grand Challenge will begin with the announcement of the problem statement. Teams will be given one month to prepare and submit a preliminary design for the Drone Forensic Toolkit.

The preliminary submission should include: Drone Forensic Toolkit architecture, forensic methodology, supported UAV platforms, digital evidence acquisition workflow, hardware acquisition interfaces, evidence preservation mechanisms, chain-of-custody methodology, cryptographic hashing approach, metadata extraction strategy, reporting framework, software architecture, risk assessment, and validation methodology.

No financial support will be provided at this stage. The objective of this stage is to identify technically promising teams with strong and feasible solutions.

#### Stage 2: Drone Forensic Toolkit Detailed Design and Prototype Development

The organizing team will take approximately two weeks to evaluate preliminary designs and shortlist up to 15 teams. These teams will receive **Rs. 1 lakh each** to develop a complete, detailed design and prototype.

This phase will last approximately two months. Teams shall prepare a Drone Forensic Toolkit capable of performing physical and logical acquisition of digital evidence from supported UAV platforms. The toolkit shall include evidence acquisition modules, forensic parsers, evidence preservation mechanisms, cryptographic hashing, chain-of-custody management, evidence repository architecture, timeline reconstruction, and automated forensic reporting.

#### Stage 3: Drone Forensic Toolkit Detailed Design and Prototype Evaluation

After submission of Drone Forensic Toolkit detailed design and prototype reports, the organizing team will take approximately two weeks to evaluate the submissions. Up to 10 teams will be shortlisted for final presentation during IIT Bombay Techfest in December 2026.

During this stage, the evaluation committee may provide brief feedback to the shortlisted teams. Teams will be encouraged to improve their design, prototype, documentation, and presentation before the final round. Travel or participation support may be provided to the shortlisted finalist teams, subject to the approved budget norms.

#### Final Presentation: Techfest Evaluation

The final round will be conducted during IIT Bombay Techfest in December 2026. Finalist teams will present their detailed design and prototype before the evaluation committee and respond to technical questions.

**The final evaluation will be based on:**
- Detailed design, prototype and demonstration.
- Supported technical evidence.
- Final presentation.
- Viva and technical defence.
- Feasibility of implementation in the next phase.

Up to 5 winning teams will be selected for Grand Challenge 3. These winners will receive awards or recognition grants and will be considered for further financial support, technical mentoring, and access to resources for prototype development and testing with IIT Bombay support.

### 1.6 Expected Target Audience

The Grand Challenge is intended for participants with expertise in digital forensics, cybersecurity, embedded systems, and UAV technologies that are capable of developing innovative forensic solutions for drone investigations. The Grand Challenge is open to academic institutions, research laboratories, startups, MSMEs, defence organizations, and industry consortia possessing expertise in digital forensics, cybersecurity, embedded systems, and UAV technologies.

The challenge aims to foster indigenous development of advanced drone forensic capabilities suitable for law enforcement agencies, forensic laboratories, regulatory bodies, and defence organizations.

Project staff, research staff, consultants, interns, and other personnel directly engaged with the PUSHPAK Project, and Drone Centre, or the organizing/host institutions in connection with the Grand Challenge are **NOT eligible** to participate.

This restriction applies whether such personnel participate individually or as part of a team. Any team found to include an ineligible participant may be disqualified at any stage of the Grand Challenge, including after selection or announcement of results. The organizers reserve the right to verify the eligibility of participants at any stage and their decision in this regard shall be final.
